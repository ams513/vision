The eleven promises

Pull the plug. It still works.

Pull the plug and thirteen of our browser apps still work. We cannot read their data, because the apps hold no code that sends it, and twelve of them ask for no account. Forget the passphrase on Family Vault, Ledger, Warden, Baseline or Fellowship and nobody can open it, not even us. Fifteen products are paid once with no subscription, every one can be tried before it is bought, and the code of the browser apps can be read in plain files. Warden never calls a message safe, the home page loads no third-party script, and there are no invented reviews or user counts. Each promise has a test a stranger can run in a minute.

III   What we are reaching for

The impossible, one picture at a time

Every arm the founder has named, as a scene to step into. Open a tile and look around. These are pictures of a future, and every one is a horizon.

01Horizon

Stark BioOrganics

Soil, seed and living materials

Picture a field at first light: dark soil, warm compost, seed saved from last year's harvest. Imagine fibres, resins and living materials grown with patience, season after season, for makers who need something that leaves the land no poorer. We are reaching for an arm of Stark whose whole craft lies in growing the raw stuff that other things come from, and growing it so well that nothing needs taking twice. One day, a log of soil and seed kept by anyone with a garden. For now, a horizon and a notebook.

02Horizon

Stark Retail goods

Natural, biodegradable things that feed off Stark bio

Imagine a shelf where every object tells you what it was made from and where it goes when you are finished with it. Picture a cup, a cloth, a tool, a toy, grown from living material and made to return to the soil. We are building toward natural, biodegradable goods that feed off Stark's own bio-grown materials, in a shop where you try first and read an honest tag before anything leaves your hand. One day, the thing you finish with becomes the thing that feeds the next one.

03Horizon

EarthShip (working name)

Homes that grow food and gather water

Picture a home at the edge of a village, warm in winter and cool in summer, with greenhouse beds along the south wall and rainwater gathered from its own roof. Imagine quiet software watching over soil, water and light, so a household feeds itself from its own walls. Imagine the plans open to everyone, so the next community can raise the next home from the last. We are building toward it, starting with a planner and a garden log. Water, structure and wiring always deserve qualified local eyes.

04Horizon

Stark Youth

No child unseen; learning through real connection

Imagine a child who never has to wonder whether anyone noticed. Picture an evening with the screens dark: a table, a project, three generations arguing happily over how to build the thing. Picture young people learning by doing real things with real people, growing into the kind of leaders others trust because they listen first. We are reaching for programmes where no child goes unseen, where learning means connection, and where leadership gets practised long before anyone needs it. Tools for the adults around a child exist today. The rest lies ahead.

05Horizon

Stark Infancy

Every child wanted, held, fed and known

Picture a world where every baby arrives to open arms. A night when a worn-out parent finds a second pair of hands, a clear list of what comes next, a neighbour at the door with soup. Picture every infant held, fed and known by name, and no mother or father left to manage alone. We are reaching for a society that wraps its youngest in attention before they ask. One day, no child born into silence. We begin where we can: a private record of feeds, sleep and who has tonight's shift.

06Horizon

Stark Maternal and Stark Paternal

Two doors into one house

Imagine two doors into the same house. One opens for a mother, one for a father, and each leads to a room that belongs wholly to the person inside. Picture those rooms standing alone, complete, private, each with its own key. Then picture the choice to join them: a shared hallway where two parents lay out only what they both decide to share, and either can walk away with everything that belongs to them. We are building toward care that adapts to the family, never care that watches over it.

07Horizon

StarkMobile and the Stark voice

A device you speak to, sealed under your key

Picture a device that fits the palm and answers to your voice alone. You speak, it listens, and whatever it holds stays sealed under your key. Imagine one calm voice running through every Stark tool, a presence that guides without watching. Picture something you carry and can put down, always outside the body and never in it, a vow written into its design. We are building toward it from a small start: a home screen that opens Stark apps with the network off. The thousand-word voice script exists on paper. The device waits beyond the horizon.

08Horizon

StarkBionics

Prosthetics and surgical advances

Imagine a hand that closes around a cup, a leg that climbs its first hill, a surgeon's instrument steadier than any tremor. Picture prosthetics and surgical advances shaped by engineers, prosthetists and surgeons working side by side, each made to fit one person and care for them. We reach toward this as repair and restoration, and only here: surgery and prosthetics form the one place Stark would ever come near the body. Years of qualified hands and careful review stand between today and that picture. We begin with a simple fitting log for people who wear a prosthesis.

09Horizon

StarkSynthetics

Adaptive, bio-friendly supports outside the body

Imagine a support that learns how its wearer moves, softens for an egg and firms for a toolbox, and rests against the skin without ever entering it. Picture assistive devices that adapt to the person instead of asking the person to adapt. We are reaching for the impossible, named out loud: adaptive, bio-friendly, non-invasive care and repair, made with those who know, and widening in time across everything Stark touches. Today, a horizon with nothing built. The first step lies in software for people who already use such devices: settings, fit and maintenance logs, visit summaries, kept on the device.

10Horizon

Stark Bioengineering

Organic synthesis, mixed and matched

Imagine a laboratory where patient people study living systems with one aim: to learn, and to repair. Picture the work shaped for organic synthesis, slow, supervised, checked by outside eyes at every turn, and kept out of the body, with surgery and prosthetics the single exception. And picture a shop where you mix and match what you need and pay a little less for choosing more. Bundles already exist in a fixed form. The laboratory sits years away and not yet begun.

11Horizon

StarkEconomy

Work that fits inside a life

Imagine a working life where the paperwork melts away. People still do meaningful tasks, but the hours once lost to forms, chasing and friction come back to them as dinner, as sleep, as a walk. Picture work that fits inside a life instead of eating it: far less work, far less stress, woven together with travel, so a week of tasks and a week of wandering can share one calendar. We are building toward an economy of lighter tasks, and the name may yet change for something better. Ledger, Trades and Career already lift one person's paperwork.

12Horizon

StarkPilgrim

Travel, tours, true facts and fun things to do

Picture a traveller stepping off a train in a city she has never seen, a pocket guide telling her what the old square once was, where the good bread hides, which path climbs to the view. Imagine tours, true facts and fun things to do gathered from every corner of the world, each source credited. Picture a lighter working life that leaves room for all of it. We are reaching for a companion for wandering, joined with StarkEconomy. Today it starts as a trip notebook: a plan, a packing list, a journal.

13Horizon

Last Shall be First

A programme against poverty

Picture a street where no one sleeps outside. A congregation sets up a care table in an afternoon, a neighbour carries a key to a first front door, and a table somewhere has one more seat at it. Imagine poverty turning into a story old people tell, outreach so quick and so simple that any shelter, church or friend can start it, with care, a home and a place among others arriving together. We are reaching for that world, in dignity and never pity, beginning with the people who are usually last. The last shall be first.

14Horizon

Prisonbreak (working name)

Rehabilitation, trust and a way back

Imagine a gate that opens onto a road instead of a wall. A person walks out of prison and into an interview that reads the gap on a resume as a chapter and never as a verdict, into housing that waits, into neighbours who offer trust. Picture a society that studies the countries doing this best, then aims past them, with fewer people back inside and trust built without any score or watch kept on anyone. We are reaching for it, with prisons, probation services and people who have lived it leading.

15Horizon

Stark Automotive

Stark tools inside vehicles and aircraft

Imagine stepping into a car, a truck or an aircraft and finding your own tools already there: your service record, your pre-trip checklist, your trip notes, tidy and yours. Picture the leading makers of vehicles and aircraft building them in alongside us, with nothing that touches how a machine steers, brakes or flies. We are reaching for a seamless handshake between Stark tools and the machines that carry people. No partner stands named, because none has signed. The first step lies in a plain vehicle record.

16Horizon

StarkNotToday

A shield: warning, shelter, safe passage

Picture a morning when the warning sounds before the storm, not after, and every phone, radio and loudspeaker in a valley carries the same calm message: go here, now, together. Imagine early warning, shelter and safe routes joined across a town, a nation, a continent, with every government at the table and emergency services leading. A shield and only a shield: warning, shelter, getting people out of harm's way. No weapon, no targeting, no retaliation, ever. We are reaching for a world that answers danger by moving people to safety.

17Horizon

StarkHolographics

Light with depth, a future you can see

Imagine light that holds a shape in the air: a model of a home you can walk around, a planet you can turn with two fingers, a friend from far away sitting across your table. Picture a showcase, a studio and a shop that already feel like that future, quiet, deep, unmistakable, with nothing on screen that shows off. We are reaching for real depth in light, and until the optics exist we call a screen effect a screen effect. Stark's own look, a single slit of cold light in the dark, already points the way.

18Horizon

StarkAI

A local assistant for the day the networks go dark

Picture the day the networks go dark. The towers fall silent, the cloud lies out of reach, and an assistant on your own machine keeps answering, listening to a small radio for news, reading from a library you stored in advance. Imagine one that admits what it cannot answer, never poses as a person, and lives wholly on your device, a receiver in its own right. We are reaching for it: local, quiet, honest about its limits, a friend in a storm and no stand-in for a doctor or an official service.

19Horizon

StarkDelivery

Hands-free and touch-locked, for the people who carry things

Picture a courier on a wet night with a phone in a pocket and both hands on the work. A voice names the next turn, a touch lock shrugs off the rain, and nothing asks the rider to look down. Imagine delivery that never pulls a driver's eyes from the road, in every city on earth, calm and cutting edge. We are building toward StarkDelivery: fully hands-free, made for the people who carry things for everyone else, with data that stays on the device. An idea recorded, a specification still to come.

20Horizon

StarkRelief

Funds and tools for the poor, the sick, the broken

Picture a ledger anyone can open, where every gift gets counted in daylight and flows to the poor, the sick, the broken, the wounded and the afflicted, everywhere on earth. Picture shelters, food banks, clinics and hospices holding the tools they need for free, asked for with one short message and no questions about why. We are reaching for relief at the scale of the need: donations to all things possible, kept honest by a count that can never run larger than the real giving. Today, a design written down. No money has moved yet.
